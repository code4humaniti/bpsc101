BPSC Mains Structure & Final Selection Cutoffs (1020 Marks)

Detailed breakdown of BPSC Mains merit papers (GS 1, GS 2, Essay) and Interview marks alongside category-wise final selection cutoffs.

📜 Mains Paper Weightage & Score Distribution

GS 1 300 Marks

General Studies Paper 1

History of India & Bihar Culture (Patna Kalam, Mauryan, Pala Art, 1857 Revolt), National/Intl Current Affairs & Statistical Analysis/Graphs.

Merit Paper • 29.4% of Total Score
GS 2 300 Marks

General Studies Paper 2

Indian Polity & Governance (Bihar Panchayati Raj, Federalism), Indian & Bihar Economy & Geography, Science & Technology Role in Development.

Merit Paper • 29.4% of Total Score
ESSAY 300 Marks

Essay Paper (3 Essays)

3 Essays (100 marks each): Section 1 (General/National), Section 2 (Socio-Economic), Section 3 (Bihar Proverbs & Folklore in Devanagari).

Merit Paper • 29.4% of Total Score
INTERVIEW 120 Marks

Personality Test

Evaluates administrative aptitude, situational judgment, Bihar state knowledge, communication skills, and general awareness.

Merit Stage • 11.8% of Total Score
QUALIFYING 100 Marks

General Hindi (सामान्य हिंदी)

Qualifying only (Pass Marks: 30%). Marks are NOT added to final merit list. Covers grammar, essay, precis, and sentence correction.

Non-Merit • Pass Required
QUALIFYING MCQ 100 Marks

Optional Paper (MCQ Format)

Qualifying MCQ paper (Pass Marks: 40% UR, 36.5% BC, 34% EBC, 32% SC/ST/Women). Marks are NOT added to final merit list.

Non-Merit • Qualifying MCQ

✍️ BPSC Essay Paper (300 Marks) Structure

Section 1 100 Marks

National & Philosophical Topics

Democracy, Good Governance, International Relations, Artificial Intelligence, Ethics, Climate Change, or Freedom Struggle.

Section 2 100 Marks

Socio-Economic & Development

Poverty alleviation, Agriculture in Bihar, Women Empowerment, Education Policy, Industrialization, or Sustainable Growth.

Section 3 (Unique) 100 Marks

Bihar Cultural Wisdom & Proverbs

Written in Devanagari script. Focuses on traditional Bihar proverbs (e.g. "बिना मवले काम ना होला", "खेती आपन सेती") and rural folklore.
